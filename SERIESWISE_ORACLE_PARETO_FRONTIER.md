# GiftEval series-wise Oracle MASE/FLOPs envelope

Updated: 2026-08-10

## Scope

This is the series-wise extension of `ORACLE_PARETO_FRONTIER.md` for
Chronos2-Small. Each of the 371,330 GiftEval forecast instances may choose its
own measured numeric context window or its native/full context. In other words,
the policy is forecast-row-wise; it is not constrained to reuse one action for
the whole dataset/term cell.

MASE remains leaderboard-faithful:

1. `mase_gluonts_real` is aggregated with its valid forecast-point counts inside
   each of the 97 dataset/term cells;
2. each cell is normalized by its published Seasonal-Naive MASE; and
3. the 97 normalized cell values are aggregated by geometric mean.

FLOPs are the sum of the theoretical TSFM inference cost over all forecast
instances. This is the physically meaningful benchmark-workload accounting for
a policy that chooses a different context per row. It follows the existing
Stage-4/Phase-6 FLOPs convention and does not add predictor overhead.

## Main result

Series-wise outcome information exposes dramatically more headroom than a
single dataset/term action:

| Policy | Normalized MASE | Workload FLOPs saved |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full/native | 0.726708 | 0.00% |
| Dataset-wise accuracy Oracle | 0.704681 | 62.21% |
| **Series-wise accuracy Oracle** | **0.621624** | **73.55%** |
| Within 0.1% of series Oracle | 0.622207 | 85.05% |
| Within 0.5% | 0.624729 | 89.62% |
| Within 1% | 0.627362 | 91.10% |
| Within 2% | 0.633935 | 92.56% |
| No worse than full/native | 0.721755 | **94.29%** |
| Minimum-compute endpoint | 1.048471 | 94.41% |

The exact minimum-MASE endpoint reproduces the independent Phase-6 instance
Oracle value, 0.621624. It improves absolute normalized MASE by 0.0831 relative
to the dataset-wise Oracle while also saving another 11.34 percentage points of
workload FLOPs. The selected Oracle context has median 192 and mean 937 tokens.

The accuracy curve is almost flat until roughly 85% saving and remains very
strong through 90%:

|---:|---:|---:|
| 75% | 75.05% | 0.621625 |
| 80% | 80.02% | 0.621695 |
| 85% | 85.05% | 0.622207 |
| 90% | 90.02% | 0.625267 |
| 92% | 92.13% | 0.631185 |
| 94% | 94.03% | 0.671779 |

## Applying Mamba series-wise

The existing Chronos2-Small Mamba checkpoint was applied to every forecast
instance using its stored per-row predicted score curve. No outcome labels are
used by these Mamba policies.

| Mamba policy | Normalized MASE | Workload FLOPs saved |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dataset-shared curve | **0.724344** | 58.03% |
| Series-wise, raw row argmin | 0.726465 | 57.81% |
| **Series-wise, 0.25 row / 0.75 cell-consensus shrinkage** | 0.724793 | **64.47%** |

The raw per-series predictions are too noisy: they are only marginally better
than full context and save slightly less compute than the dataset-shared policy.
Shrinking each row's score curve toward its label-free dataset/term consensus
fixes most of that variance. The regularized series policy gives up only
0.000449 normalized MASE (0.062% relative) versus dataset-shared Mamba while
saving an additional 6.45 percentage points of workload FLOPs.

The original Pareto-overlay reconstruction reported 0.727328 at 54.20% because
it unconditionally recycled the last-grid prediction as a native score. The
Phase-6 evaluator correctly disables that synthetic native score in cells where
Stage 3 did not supervise it; the corrected raw-policy result above is
authoritative. The local analysis script now applies the same guard, but the
previously generated plot/CSV overlay should be regenerated before publication.

The learned policy is nevertheless far from the series Oracle. At greater
compute saving, the Oracle obtains 0.621624 versus Mamba's 0.724793, a 16.60%
relative MASE gap. Series-specific action prediction, rather than action-grid
coverage, is therefore the dominant opportunity and the dominant difficulty.

## Frontier qualification

The minimum-MASE and minimum-compute endpoints are exact. Exhaustively
enumerating the full discrete frontier is infeasible: the action space is on the
order of 19 choices raised to hundreds of thousands of rows, while the official
geometric aggregation couples rows within each dataset/term cell.

The plotted curve is consequently a reproducible **feasible nondominated
envelope**, not a proof of the exhaustive discrete frontier. It contains 201
nondominated policies selected from 295 policies generated by per-series
relative-regret constraints and two global FLOPs scalarization sweeps that
linearize the official log-MASE objective around the full and accuracy-Oracle
solutions. Every plotted policy is attainable; still-better unsupported
combinations may exist.
